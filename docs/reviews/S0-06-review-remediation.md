## 3. The three findings of Review 3

#### R3-01 — MAJOR — Legacy writers must stay fenced during recovery and restore (final focused review: RESOLVED)

- **Original requirement (focused re-review, R3-01).** "The cutover now requires a repeat reconnect check, treats `application_name` as observability, and uses row identities for completeness detection. The recovery table nevertheless permits lifting the legacy fence after holding only the audit-capable writers. That leaves a path for the old build to resume unaudited writes. Require any restored pre-activation build to remain unable to write. Also make the fence's access check cover **effective** privileges, including inherited or assumable roles and access to the new writer credentials. A negative test using only the old connection string does not cover an overlapping deployment that receives the replacement credential. Specify sufficient schema and domain `SELECT` privileges for the positive audited-writer control, since the existing repository reads domain rows and uses `RETURNING`." Evidence cited by Codex: specification line 378, the recovery exception at line 415, and `drizzle-investigation-repository.ts` line 79.
- **Owner direction (nine safety invariants, recorded as register C15, not as an approval of D1 to D9 or A5).** (1) Once mandatory audit is activated, legacy unaudited writers stay fenced throughout operation, recovery, rollback and restoration. (2) No recovery procedure restores unaudited mutation access. (3) Restoring an old build does not authorize it to mutate. (4) If safe audited writes cannot be restored, maintain WRITE HOLD. (5) Evaluate effective privileges: direct grants, inherited privileges, assumable roles, ownership and access to replacement credentials. (6) Replacement credentials are not available to legacy deployments. (7) Negative verification covers overlapping legacy deployments attempting to obtain or use replacement credentials. (8) Specify sufficient schema, table, `SELECT`, mutation and `RETURNING`-related privileges for the positive control, preserving least privilege. (9) `application_name` is an observability aid, not an authorization boundary. No exception may weaken these.
- **Latest remaining finding (Codex, restore window; the only open item after the R3-02 and R3-03 resolutions).** "The recovery table calls for WRITE HOLD and re-verification after a backup or point-in-time restore. The hold definition only deactivates the audited writer role or suspends its grants. If the restored state re-enables a legacy login or its write privileges, that hold leaves the legacy path open while checks run. This violates C15's requirement to keep legacy writers fenced throughout restoration. Require isolation from application and job connections before a restored target becomes reachable. Keep that isolation until F1 is re-established and verified. Test a legacy reconnect during the restore window, as well as after verification. Carry the requirement into WP-05 and AC-12." Evidence cited by Codex at `2854c8c`: specification lines 427 (WRITE HOLD definition), 440 (restore procedure), 475 (restore test).
- **Owner direction for the restore window (extends C15 as SI-10; no new decision or subsystem).** "Isolation Before Restore, Verification Before Release": isolate the restore or PITR target before application processes, legacy deployments, workers, scheduled jobs or pools can reach it; the mechanism is independent of restored roles, grants and credentials; only authorized recovery administration connects; existing sessions and new connections cannot bypass it; the target stays isolated until F1 (effective privileges and replacement-credential isolation) is re-established and verified; if that cannot be shown, WRITE HOLD and no exposure to application traffic; release only audit-capable writers after verification; do not rely solely on restored roles, `NOLOGIN`, revoked grants or `application_name`. The mechanism is chosen in WP-05 provided it is demonstrably enforceable.

#### R3-02 — MAJOR — UNKNOWN means hold and escalate, with no resubmission (latest Codex disposition: RESOLVED)

- **Original requirement (focused re-review, R3-02).** "The draft correctly keeps a unique candidate UNKNOWN when it cannot tie the entity to the specific request. It also specifies the wrong-operation and empty-search tests. The next paragraph permits a signed operator resubmission while acknowledging that the original may have committed. That conflicts with the settled-outcome requirement and WP-05. Keep UNKNOWN as hold and escalate. If resubmission is supported, require affirmative evidence that the specific original create did not commit and can no longer commit. A missing row, a unique candidate, or acceptance of duplicate risk does not meet that rule. Clarify that apparent uniqueness of a serial number or external reference in stored rows is insufficient unless independent evidence establishes that only the original request supplied it." Evidence cited by Codex: classification and operator procedure at specification line 424, tests at line 442, WP-05 at plan line 94.
- **Owner direction (recorded as register C16, not as an approval of D1 to D9).** (1) UNKNOWN means HOLD AND ESCALATE. (2) No automatic or manual resubmission while UNKNOWN. (3) Operator signatures, risk acceptance and administrative approval cannot override UNKNOWN. (4) COMMITTED requires affirmative evidence tied to the specific original operation. (5) NOT_COMMITTED requires affirmative evidence that it did not commit and can no longer commit. (6) Only a settled NOT_COMMITTED permits resubmission, under the normal command contract. (7) A missing row, a unique candidate, an apparently unique serial number, an external reference or the correlation id alone is insufficient. (8) Add negative acceptance cases for attempted operator override and unsupported uniqueness assumptions. (9) No idempotency subsystem. If evidence is unavailable in Phase 1 the operation remains UNKNOWN indefinitely.
